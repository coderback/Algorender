<Section step="hook" title="Two kinds of number">

135 minutes is how many hours? "2.25" is one answer; "2 hours and 15 minutes" is another, and often the one you want.
Python can give you either, because it has two kinds of number and three ways to divide. Knowing which one you are
getting saves a lot of puzzled staring at output.

</Section>

<Section step="parts" title="The pieces you will see">

<Terms>
  <Term name="int">A whole number: `7`, `0`, `-3`. Ints are exact, however big they get.</Term>
  <Term name="float">A number with a decimal point: `2.5`, `7.0`. Floats are very close, but not always exact.</Term>
  <Term name="operator">A symbol that works something out from two values, like `+` or `*`.</Term>
</Terms>

```python
print(7 + 2)    # 9     add
print(7 - 2)    # 5     subtract
print(7 * 2)    # 14    multiply
print(7 / 2)    # 3.5   divide, keeping the fraction: always a float
print(7 // 2)   # 3     divide, keeping the whole part
print(7 % 2)    # 1     the remainder after //
print(2 ** 3)   # 8     raise to a power: 2 × 2 × 2
```

Even `8 / 2` gives `4.0`, a float, because `/` always does. `//` rounds down: `-7 // 2` is `-4`, the whole number
below `-3.5`.

Python follows the order from school maths: `**` first, then `*`, `/`, `//` and `%`, then `+` and `-`. So `2 + 3 * 4`
is `14`. Brackets go first of all: `(2 + 3) * 4` is `20`.

</Section>

<Section step="see" title="Watch it run">

`//` and `%` together split minutes into hours and minutes: `135 // 60` is the 2 whole hours, and `135 % 60` is the
15 minutes left over. Step through, and watch the last line.

<TraceCode snippet="py-numbers" />

`0.1 + 0.2` prints `0.30000000000000004`. That isn't a bug in Python. Floats are stored in binary, and 0.1 has no exact
binary form, just as 1/3 has no exact decimal form (0.333…). The tiny error is normal for floats in every language;
it is why money is often counted in whole pence or cents.

<KeyIdea>

**`/` always gives a float. `//` gives the whole part and `%` the remainder.** Use ints when you need exact whole
numbers, and expect floats to be very close rather than exact.

</KeyIdea>

</Section>

want a whole number, ask for it with `//`, and get the remainder with `%`.

<Section step="recap" title="What to remember">

- Ints are whole and exact; floats have a decimal point and are very close but not always exact.
- `/` always gives a float. `//` keeps the whole part, `%` gives the remainder, `**` raises to a power.
- `**` first, then `*` `/` `//` `%`, then `+` `-`; brackets change the order.
