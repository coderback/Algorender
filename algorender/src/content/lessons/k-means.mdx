<Section step="hook" title="Groups nobody labelled">

A shop has thousands of customers and no idea how they split into types. A photo has a million colours and you want a
palette of eight. There are no labels to learn from, only points: you want the algorithm to find the groups itself.

k-means is the classic answer, and it fits in two lines: give every point to its nearest centre, then move every centre
to the middle of its points. Repeat.

</Section>

<Section step="parts" title="The pieces you will see">

<Terms>
  <Term name="centroid">The centre of a cluster. k-means keeps $k$ of them, named C1, C2, …</Term>
  <Term name="assign step">Every point joins its nearest centroid. The shading shows each centroid's territory.</Term>
  <Term name="update step">Every centroid moves to the mean of the points that joined it.</Term>
  <Term name="inertia">The sum of squared distances from each point to its centroid: how tight the clusters are. Lower is better.</Term>
</Terms>

</Section>

<Section step="see" title="Assign, move, repeat">

<Explore slug="k-means" settings={{ preset: 'blobs' }} caption="The three centroids start bunched in the middle. Each round they move to the mean of their points, and points switch sides." />

Both steps can only lower the inertia. Joining the *nearest* centroid can only shorten a point's distance, and the mean
is exactly the spot with the smallest total squared distance to a group of points. A quantity that keeps falling and
can't go below zero must settle, so k-means always stops, here after a few rounds.

But stopping isn't the same as finding the best answer:

<Explore slug="k-means" settings={{ preset: 'bad-start' }} caption="Two centroids start in the same blob. They end up sharing it, while the third stretches over the other two blobs." />

<KeyIdea>

**k-means always converges, but only to a local optimum.** Where it ends depends on where the centroids start. In
practice it is run from several starts and the result with the lowest inertia is kept; k-means++ chooses starts that
are spread out, which makes bad starts rare.

</KeyIdea>

</Section>

- **Bad start:** compare its final inertia with Three blobs: same points, much worse answer.

- **Uneven sizes:** the boundary sits halfway between the centroids, so a big, spread-out cluster loses points to a small one.

<Section step="recap" title="What to remember">

- k-means alternates two steps: assign each point to its nearest centroid, move each centroid to its mean.
- Inertia never rises, so it always stops, but possibly at a local optimum: use several starts or k-means++.
- It always finds $k$ clusters and prefers round, similar-sized ones; choosing $k$ is up to you.

</Section>
