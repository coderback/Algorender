<Section step="hook" title="A loop for every turn">

A times table is rows of columns. A seating plan is rows of seats. To print every pair of players, you pick a first
player and then, for each one, every second player. Each of these is a loop inside a loop, and once you see how the
two take turns, you can build any grid.

</Section>

<Section step="parts" title="The pieces you will see">

<Terms>
  <Term name="outer loop">The loop on the outside. Each of its turns runs the whole inner loop.</Term>
  <Term name="inner loop">The loop indented inside the outer one. It starts again from the beginning on every outer turn.</Term>
</Terms>

```python
for row in range(1, 4):
    for col in range(1, 5):
        print("*", end="")
    print()           # ends the row: inside the outer loop, after the inner one
```

This prints 3 rows of 4 stars. For `row` 1, the inner loop runs `col` through 1, 2, 3, 4; then `print()` ends the line.
Then `row` becomes 2 and the inner loop starts again from 1. The innermost line runs 3 × 4 = 12 times in all: outer
turns times inner turns. That is why loops inside loops get slow quickly: two loops of 1,000 turns each make a million.

Where a line sits matters. Indented under the inner loop, it runs once per star. Under the outer loop only, once per
row. At the left edge, once in all.

`break` inside the inner loop leaves the **inner** loop only. The outer loop carries on with its next turn.

</Section>

<Section step="see" title="Watch the loops take turns">

Step through and watch `row` and `col`. `col` runs 1, 2, 3 for every value of `row`, and `line` starts empty again at
each new row.

<KeyIdea>

**The inner loop runs all the way through for every turn of the outer loop**, so its body runs outer × inner times.
`break` leaves only the loop it is in.

</KeyIdea>

</Section>

<Misconception
  id="inner-once"
  title="The inner loop runs once, alongside the outer one"
  probes={[
    {
      q: 'for i in range(3), and inside it for j in range(4): print("x"). How many x’s?',
      choices: ['12', '7', '4'],
      answer: 0,
      trap: 1,
      why: 'The inner loop runs its 4 turns for each of the 3 outer turns: 3 × 4 = 12.',
    },
    {
      q: 'Two loops print a grid of 3 rows with 6 stars in each. How many times does the line that prints one star run?',
      choices: ['18', '9', '6'],
      answer: 0,
      trap: 1,
      why: 'The star loop runs all 6 of its turns in each of the 3 rows: 3 × 6 = 18.',
    },
  ]}
>

The inner loop doesn't run alongside the outer one, sharing its turns. It runs completely, from its first turn to its
last, inside **each** turn of the outer loop. So the work multiplies: 3 outer turns of 4 inner turns is 12, not 7.

</Misconception>

<Misconception
  id="break-leaves-all"
  title="break leaves every loop it is inside"
  probes={[
    {
      q: 'for i in range(2), inside it for j in range(3): if j == 1, break; otherwise print(i, j). What prints?',
      choices: ['0 0, then 1 0', '0 0 only', '0 0, 0 1, 0 2'],
      answer: 0,
      trap: 1,
      why: 'break leaves the inner loop only, so the outer loop goes on to i = 1, and the inner loop starts again.',
    },
    {
      q: 'An inner loop finds what it is looking for and breaks. What happens next?',
      choices: ['The outer loop carries on with its next turn', 'Both loops stop', 'The whole program ends'],
      answer: 0,
      trap: 1,
      why: 'break leaves only the loop it is in. The outer loop goes on, and starts the inner loop again on its next turn.',
    },
  ]}
>

`break` leaves the innermost loop it is in, nothing more. The outer loop carries on with its next turn, and starts the
inner loop again. To stop everything, the outer loop needs its own `break`, for example after checking a flag the
inner loop set.

</Misconception>

<Section step="recap" title="What to remember">

- The inner loop runs completely, from the start, on every turn of the outer loop.
- The innermost body runs outer × inner times, so nested loops grow quickly.
- Indentation decides whether a line runs per item, per row, or once.
- `break` leaves only the innermost loop.

</Section>
