<Section step="hook" title="Yes or no">

Is the password long enough? Is the player out of lives? Is it the weekend? Programs ask questions like these all the
time, and every answer is one of two values: `True` or `False`. Next topic, `if` will use them to decide what to do;
this one is about asking the questions.

</Section>

<Section step="parts" title="The pieces you will see">

<Terms>
  <Term name="bool">The type of `True` and `False`, the two truth values. They are written with capitals and no quotes.</Term>
  <Term name="comparison">A question that gives a bool: `==` equal, `!=` not equal, and `<`, `>`, `<=`, `>=`.</Term>
  <Term name="and, or, not">Combine bools. `and` needs both sides true, `or` needs at least one, `not` flips one.</Term>
</Terms>

```python
age = 15
print(age >= 13)                 # True
print(age == 15)                 # True: == compares
print(age >= 13 and age <= 19)   # True: both hold
print(age < 5 or age > 65)       # False: neither holds
print(not age > 18)              # True
```

Watch the difference between `=` and `==`. One `=` points a name at a value; two compare. A single `=` where a
question belongs, as in `print(5 = 5)`, stops the program with a `SyntaxError`.

`not age > 18` means `not (age > 18)`: the comparison is worked out first, then `not` flips its answer.

Values of other types count as true or false too. `0`, `0.0` and the empty string `""` count as false; every other
number and every non-empty string counts as true, even `"False"` and `"0"`. And `False` itself, of course, counts as false.

One trap: `or` joins two whole conditions. `day == "Sat" or "Sun"` doesn't ask whether the day is either one. It is
`(day == "Sat") or "Sun"`, and when the first half is false, `or` simply gives back `"Sun"`. Write each comparison in
full: `day == "Sat" or day == "Sun"`.

</Section>

`or` doesn't share the `x ==` between its sides. `x == 1 or 2` is `(x == 1) or 2`, and the lone `2` counts as true,

<Section step="recap" title="What to remember">

- A comparison gives `True` or `False`: `==`, `!=`, `<`, `>`, `<=`, `>=`.
- `and` needs both, `or` needs either, `not` flips.
- `=` stores, `==` compares.
- Each side of `or` is a whole condition: `x == 1 or x == 2`.
- `0` and `""` count as false; other numbers and non-empty strings count as true.

</Section>
